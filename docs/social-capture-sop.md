# Social Capture — How to Run the One Manual Part

Everything else in Market Pulse runs itself. Google finds and tracks competitors
in each market; Reddit pulls automatically; scoring and alerts update on their
own. **The only thing a human does is log the handful of Facebook/Nextdoor posts
worth tracking** — because no tool can pull those legally on its own.

This is a small, repeatable task. Done right it's about 5–10 minutes, a few times
a week, per market. It is not a daily grind and it is not "read the comments."

## The routine

**Frequency:** 2–3 times a week is plenty. Also do a pass in the week after any
marketing push (parade, sponsorship, mailer) so you can see if mentions moved.

**Who:** whoever is closest to the phones (a CSR works well). It does not need to
be the owner.

**What to open:** only your short watch list — the 5–10 community groups per
market plus your Nextdoor neighborhoods. You do **not** scroll all of Facebook.
Turn on new-post notifications in those specific groups so the posts come to you.

**What to capture (only these):**
1. Someone asking for a garage-door recommendation or repair.
2. A competitor being recommended or praised.
3. A complaint about a competitor (a customer you could win).
4. Anyone mentioning Inside Track.

Skip everything else. If a post isn't one of those four, it's noise — leave it.

## How to log one (the Add Social Post screen)

1. Open the **Add Social Post** page.
2. **Pick the market** (Memphis or Jackson). This is the important step — it's
   what keeps each location's numbers separate.
3. **Paste the post text** exactly as written.
4. Set the platform and, if you know it, the group name and city.
5. Tap a request type and urgency if it's obvious (optional — the AI fills in
   what you skip).
6. Check the box if Inside Track was mentioned.
7. Hit **Save & analyze**. The system stores it, then automatically figures out
   which competitors were named, the sentiment, and whether you should respond —
   and raises an alert if it's worth your attention.

That's it. If a reply is worth making, a suggested draft shows up for you to
approve, edit, and copy — you paste it yourself. The tool never posts.

## Rules (keep it clean and safe)

- Only capture posts from groups you're a legitimate member of. You're acting as
  yourself, by hand — that's the line that keeps everything above-board.
- Paste what's publicly visible to you. Don't log private messages.
- Don't copy more personal info than needed (a first name is fine; don't collect
  phone numbers or addresses).
- When in doubt whether a post matters, skip it. Under-capturing costs nothing;
  the automated Google data carries the popularity picture on its own.

## What you get for the effort

Each logged post sharpens the popularity scores and feeds the "who's being
recommended, where" view for that market. Skip a week and nothing breaks — the
Google-based tracking keeps running. The manual layer is the bonus that turns
"who has the most reviews" into "who neighbors actually name."
